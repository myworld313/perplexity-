# Eight-Week CIPP/US Study Plan

## Intended learner

This plan is designed for a financial-services compliance professional moving toward privacy, cybersecurity GRC, technology risk, or vendor-risk work. It builds on strengths in regulated operations, customer due diligence, documentation, control execution, and risk-based decisions.

## Study schedule

| Week | Focus | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | U.S. privacy framework and enforcement | Explain sectoral privacy law, FTC authority, state roles, and core principles |
| 2 | Financial and consumer data | Connect GLBA, FCRA, identity theft, notices, safeguards, and sharing |
| 3 | Health, education, children, communications, and marketing | Distinguish HIPAA, COPPA, FERPA, TCPA, CAN-SPAM, and tracking issues |
| 4 | Government and court access; workplace privacy | Analyze subpoenas, preservation, monitoring, screening, and employee-data scenarios |
| 5 | Comprehensive state privacy laws | Map scope, rights, notices, consent, opt-outs, contracts, assessments, and enforcement |
| 6 | California and state-law outliers | Focus on California, biometrics, health data, breach laws, and state developments |
| 7 | Practice questions and weak-area remediation | Turn errors into a focused revision list |
| 8 | Final review and exam readiness | Use timed practice and refresh the official blueprint |

## Weekly method

1. Read the applicable official Body of Knowledge section first.
2. Create a one-page law map: scope, data, obligations, rights, enforcement, and exceptions.
3. Apply the topic to a realistic business scenario.
4. Complete practice questions and record why each missed answer was incorrect.
5. Revisit the prior week's map before beginning the next section.

## Example scenario

A payments company gives a marketing vendor consumer contact information and online behavioral data. Identify the data, relevant financial-services and state privacy requirements, vendor-contract duties, notice and opt-out implications, and the needed security, retention, and oversight controls.

## Career translation

Present the credential with operational experience, for example: "Applied privacy-law and consumer-data-governance principles to regulated financial-services operations, supporting data-use controls, third-party oversight, customer communications, and risk-based compliance decisions."

## Readiness checklist

- I can identify the sectoral law or state-law overlay in a fact pattern.
- I can distinguish collection, use, disclosure, retention, security, and breach-notification issues.
- I can explain consumer rights, employee privacy, and vendor obligations.
- I can apply structured issue spotting under timed conditions.
- I have reviewed the most recent official IAPP Body of Knowledge and Exam Blueprint.
