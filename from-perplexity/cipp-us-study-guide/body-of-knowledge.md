# CIPP/US Current Body of Knowledge

## What the exam tests

CIPP/US covers the U.S. privacy environment, limits on private-sector collection and use of personal information, government and court access, workplace privacy, and state privacy and data-security laws. The current IAPP Body of Knowledge and Exam Blueprint are the definitive sources for tested content.

## 1. U.S. privacy environment

### Legal foundation

- Constitutional, statutory, common-law, and administrative-law sources of privacy obligations.
- Federal and state roles, preemption, and overlapping legal requirements.
- The sectoral architecture of U.S. privacy law.

### Enforcement

- Federal Trade Commission authority and unfair or deceptive acts or practices.
- State attorneys general, specialized federal regulators, consent orders, litigation, and available private rights of action.
- How enforcement priorities shape privacy-program controls.

### Privacy principles

- Notice, choice, access, correction, accountability, data minimization, purpose limitation, security, retention, and disposal.
- Data inventories, vendor oversight, and governance controls.

## 2. Private-sector collection and use

### Consumer-protection baseline

- Privacy representations, consent, online tracking, advertising practices, data security, and FTC expectations.
- The difference between published promises and actual operations.

### Sectoral laws

- Financial services: GLBA, Safeguards Rule concepts, FCRA, identity-theft protections, and financial-data obligations.
- Health: HIPAA privacy and security concepts, covered entities, business associates, and protected health information.
- Children and education: COPPA, FERPA, parental consent, and student-information protections.
- Communications and marketing: TCPA, CAN-SPAM, behavioral advertising, and online tracking.

## 3. Government and court access

- Law-enforcement requests, preservation demands, disclosure exceptions, and records handling.
- National-security and surveillance-related privacy concerns.
- Civil discovery, subpoenas, litigation holds, and voluntary versus compelled disclosure.

## 4. Workplace privacy

- Applicant, employee, contractor, and former-employee data.
- Background checks, monitoring, investigations, biometrics, communications, and retention.
- State-law overlays, notice requirements, and proportionality.

## 5. State privacy and security laws

### Comprehensive state-law framework

- Applicability thresholds and exemptions.
- Personal information, sensitive data, deidentified data, and pseudonymous data.
- Consumer rights: access, deletion, correction, portability, opt-out, appeal, and non-discrimination.
- Notices, consent, opt-out preference signals, sales/sharing, targeted advertising, profiling, and automated decision-making.
- Data-protection assessments, processor or service-provider contracts, security, enforcement, and private-right-of-action differences.

### Important state-specific areas

- California privacy-law structure and California Privacy Protection Agency role.
- Biometric privacy, facial-recognition limits, consumer-health-data laws, data brokers, children’s privacy, tracking, and AI-related requirements.
- Data-breach notification: common elements, timing, notice recipients, and material state differences.

## Scenario method

For any fact pattern, identify the entity and industry, data type, collection or disclosure purpose, applicable law or state overlay, affected rights, and the control or response that best addresses the issue.
